# Nanna / Ningal

Sumerian moon is not Selene. Selene is Greek.

## Nanna / Suen / Sin

The moon itself. Sumerian **Nanna** (also **Suen**). Akkadian later says **Sin**.

- Son of Enlil and Ninlil.
- City god of Ur. Temple Ekišnugal.
- Sign: recumbent crescent. Animal: bull.
- Written as the number **30** — days in the month.

That last tick is the two-worlds move: the name is a number.

## Ningal

The moon *goddess* in this system. Sumerian **Nin-gal**, “Great Queen.” Akkadian **Nikkal**.

- Wife of Nanna/Suen.
- Lady / mother of Ur.
- Children include Utu (sun) and Inanna (Venus) — not the moon.

Ningal is the consort, not the disk. The disk is Nanna. Both belong to the same house.

## Against Selene

| Sky-word | Culture | Sex of the moon-body |
|---|---|---|
| Selene / Mene | Greek | goddess *is* the moon |
| Nanna / Suen / Sin | Sumer / Akkad | god *is* the moon |
| Ningal | Sumer | goddess beside the moon |

Same object in the day-sky photo. Different grammar of the name.

## Fork (poetry, not grammar)

Do not change the Sumerian: *nin* = lady, *gal* = great.

Ear-fork in English:

**Ningal** → *nin* + *gal* → nine + gal → **the girl of the number nine**.

Nanna is already a number (30). This fork gives Ningal a number too. Nine: last single digit. 3×3. Completes the line before ten.

Nine stays charged after Sumer — Greek nine Muses, Jewish nine as a full small count, Arabic 99 names. Babylon used 9 in late math tables (powers of nine), but the moon-sign itself stayed **30**. The nine is the fork, not the tablet.
